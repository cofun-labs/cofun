# Notico privacy

Notico is a Chrome extension that opens an AI side panel in Chrome, which reads and edits your Notion pages with you, and can read the page you are on when it is another site and web pages whose address you give it, and search the web. It is one of the [Cofun](../../README.md) extensions, which share one server and one sign-in. This page says what data Notico reads, where that data goes, and what is kept on your device. What the server and the sign-in do with it, the same for every Cofun extension, is on [Cofun's privacy page](../../PRIVACY.md); the two pages together are Notico's privacy policy. They describe the extension built from this folder with its committed configuration (`extensions/notico/.env`) and the worker deployed from `worker/`. If you build the extension against your own deployment, the hosts are the ones you configure.

The short version: when you ask a question, the conversation, including the Notion content and the text of other web pages the assistant read to answer it, goes to our worker and from there to an AI model that runs on Cloudflare. If you set up your own model on OpenRouter, it goes to OpenRouter instead, with your own key, and not to our worker. We do not store that content on our servers. Your chats are saved on your device only. Unless you turn it off in the settings, Notico also tells us which of its features are used and whether they worked, never what you asked, what the assistant answered, or anything from your pages.

## What the extension reads

What leaves your browser, by kind of data. Each kind goes to the hosts listed under [Where data goes](#where-data-goes). What is read for a question (Notion content, tab titles and URLs, the text of pages in your tabs, text sent with "Ask Notico", and web pages and searches) becomes part of **your conversation**, which goes to our worker and the AI model (or to OpenRouter, if you answer with your own model there) and is saved with the chat on your device, as described below. The sign-in session, credits, the OpenRouter key, and usage data never join the conversation.

- **Notion content, read as you.** To answer a question, the assistant reads from Notion with your own Notion session, in your Notion tab: pages (as text), database rows and properties, comment threads, search results, the list of pages in your workspace, what you have selected in the page, and the names of workspace members when you ask it to mention someone. It reads only what a question needs, and it can read anything your Notion account can. When you type at least two characters in the page picker, the text you typed is sent to Notion's search, from your Notion tab, to find pages in your workspace.
- **Changes to Notion, made as you.** When you ask it to, the assistant edits and creates pages, changes properties, adds comments, and moves pages. When you save a reply or a chat to Notion yourself, it creates a page under the page open in the current tab, holding your questions and the assistant's replies. These are requests to Notion, sent from your Notion tab.
- **Tab addresses and titles.** The side panel opens on every tab, and you can chat on any of them; the Notion tools work only while the current tab is a Notion page (`www.notion.so` or `app.notion.com`). With each question, and each time the assistant reads or changes Notion, the extension looks at the URL of the current tab (the active tab of the panel's window) to tell whether it is Notion. What leaves the browser:
  - the title and URL of the open Notion page, with every question;
  - the title and URL of each Notion page or tab you add to a question yourself (with `@` or the "+" button in the message box), with that question; of a Notion page, not its content: the assistant reads what it needs of it, as above;
  - the title and URL of the current tab on another site, only when a question needs them and the assistant looks them up (say, "save a link to this page in my reading list"). On a tab that is not Notion, a question otherwise says only that the current tab is not a Notion page.

  A URL is the whole address, including its query string and fragment, which on some sites hold a sign-in or share token. When you open the page picker, the extension reads the URLs and titles of your tabs that show a Notion page, and of the tabs in the side panel's window on other sites, to offer them; they stay in the browser unless you add one.
- **Text of the pages in your tabs, on sites you allowed.** The assistant can read the page in the current tab when a question is about it (say, "summarize this article"), and the page of a tab you added to a chat. On a site other than Notion it needs your permission: the first time, the side panel asks you to allow Notico to read that site, and Chrome asks you again; nothing of the page is read before you said yes in both. Adding a tab on a site you have not allowed asks Chrome to let Notico read it, and the tab is added only once you said yes. Once allowed, the extension may read that site's pages in your tabs until you remove the access in Chrome's extension settings (Site access). It reads, inside the tab, the main content of the page as it shows it, as Markdown (its headings, lists, tables, and the addresses its links point to, but no image addresses), with the page's title and URL, up to a limit, and only when the assistant reads it for a question. That includes what the page shows you because you are signed in there. No request is sent to the site: the text is read from the page already open in the tab. A tab you added can be read in that question or a later one of the same chat, for as long as the tab is open and the browser has not restarted; what is read is the page the tab shows then, even if the tab went to another page since you added it, and its title and URL go with it.
- **Text you select and send with "Ask Notico".** When you select text on a page, on any site, Notion included, right-click it, and pick "Ask Notico", the extension reads the selected text inside that tab (Chrome lets it, for that tab, because you clicked the menu item; no site needs to be allowed), with the page's title and URL, up to a limit. It shows in the side panel's message box, where you can take it off, and leaves the browser only with the next question you send. Until then it is kept in the browser's memory for the side panel (`chrome.storage.session`), which Chrome clears when the browser closes, and nothing is sent anywhere. The question also keeps the page's address and the start of the text, to show the selection with it.
- **Web pages read by their address, and web searches.** When a question needs a web page (say, you write "summarize https://example.com/article", or a page the assistant read links to another), the assistant can have our worker read it by its address, plainly or rendered in a browser for a page drawn by scripts; it chooses the address: one you gave, a link it found in a page or in Notion, or one it thinks of. When a question needs current information or sources beyond your Notion and the pages you gave, it can have our worker search the web, with a query it writes, which may hold text from your Notion pages or the conversation. The extension sends the address or the query to the worker ([Cofun's privacy page](../../PRIVACY.md#who-receives-your-data) says who the worker passes it to), and the page's title and text, or each result's address, title, and a description of its page, come back into the conversation. This goes through our worker whichever model answers, your own included. Nothing of such a page is read from your browser: a page you see only because you are signed in can't be read this way.
- **Your sign-in session.** You sign in on a web page served by our worker, with [Clerk](https://clerk.com). The extension reads the Clerk session cookies that signing in there sets to know who is signed in, and gets a short-lived session token from Clerk for each request to the worker. The side panel shows your name, email address, and avatar from that account.
- **Your credits.** To show your credits, the extension asks the worker for them, with how far your clock is from UTC, so that a day is a day on your clock; to buy credits, which pack you chose.
- **Your OpenRouter key, if you set one up.** It is sent only to OpenRouter.
- **Usage data**, unless you turn it off: see [Usage data](#usage-data).

It never reads the content of tabs other than the current one and the tabs you added to a chat, or the content of a site you did not allow (other than text you selected and sent with "Ask Notico"). It never reads your browsing history, or what you type outside the side panel.

## Where data goes

The extension sends requests to these hosts and no others. The extension's content security policy stops its own pages from connecting anywhere else, and a test pins that policy and the hosts the extension has permission for, so neither can change unnoticed.

| Host | What is sent | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `www.notion.so`, `app.notion.com` | Requests to Notion's own API, sent from inside your Notion tab with that tab's session: reads, searches (the assistant's, and the text you type in the page picker), and the changes you ask for, saved chats and replies included | To read and edit your pages. This is the same service your Notion tab already talks to |
| `api.cofun.cc` (our worker) | Your Clerk session token, the extension's name and version, a random id for the chat and one for the question (see [Your chats](#what-stays-on-your-device)), and for each question your conversation so far: your messages, the assistant's replies and instructions, and everything the question used of the kinds above (Notion content, tab titles and URLs, the text of pages in your tabs and of text you sent with "Ask Notico", web pages read by their address, and searches with their results) | To get the AI model's reply |
| `api.cofun.cc` (our worker) | Your Clerk session token, the extension's name and version, the random ids of the chat and the question it is for, and the address of a web page the assistant chose to read (with how much of its text to return), or the query of a web search it chose to make (with how many results to return) | To read the page, plainly or rendered, or make the search, for the assistant; what comes back then goes with the conversation. Also when your own model answers |
| `api.cofun.cc` (our worker) | Your Clerk session token; how far your clock is from UTC, in minutes, and, when you read further down your credit history, the place in the list to go on from (both as part of the request's address); and when you buy credits, which pack you chose | To show you your own credits, how many you used each day, by the days of your own clock, and their history; to list the packs on offer, and to start a checkout for the one you chose |
| `openrouter.ai` (OpenRouter), only once you switch it on in the settings | When you check your OpenRouter key in the settings: the key, to OpenRouter's key endpoint, which says whether it works and what it has left, and asks no model anything. Once a key is saved and OpenRouter is on, when the settings page shows it: a request for its public list of models, with nothing of yours in it, once each time the side panel opens, and again after a request that failed. When you ask a question with one of its models chosen: your OpenRouter key, and the conversation so far, as listed for the worker above, without the chat and question ids | To get the reply from the model you chose, under your own OpenRouter account. OpenRouter passes the conversation to the company that runs that model; [OpenRouter's privacy policy](https://openrouter.ai/privacy) says what they keep. These questions do not go to our worker and spend no credits on the model; web pages and searches still go through our worker, as above |
| `us.i.posthog.com` ([PostHog](https://posthog.com), US region), unless you turn usage data off in the settings | Usage events, as listed under [Usage data](#usage-data): that something happened, with names from a fixed list, counts, and yes/no values, under a random id for your device and, once you are signed in, your Clerk user id. Never your messages, the assistant's replies, Notion content, a page's title or URL, or a key | To see how Notico is used and where it fails, so we can improve it |
| `clerk.cofun.cc` (Clerk's Frontend API) | Your Clerk session, to load your account and get session tokens | Sign-in |
| `img.clerk.com` (Clerk's image host) | A request for your account's avatar image | To show who is signed in, in the side panel's account menu and Settings page |

A site you allow Notico to read is not on this list: the extension reads its page inside your tab and sends it no request (the content security policy does not let the extension's own pages contact it).

The extension also holds a host permission for `*.cofun.cc`. It uses it for nothing but reading one cookie, and sends no requests to any host under it other than the worker and Clerk's Frontend API above: Chrome only lets an extension see a cookie when it has permission for the cookie's domain, and Clerk sets the cookie that says whether you are signed in on the sign-in page's parent domain.

Notion content never goes to Clerk. Your OpenRouter key is sent only to OpenRouter: it never goes to our worker or to Clerk. Your Notion session is never sent anywhere but Notion: requests to Notion are made inside your Notion tab, and the worker and Clerk do not receive your Notion cookies or credentials. The extension reads your Notion user id from the tab to make changes as you.

As with any web service, Cloudflare (which runs the worker), Clerk, and PostHog see your IP address when the extension connects to them. Usage events tell PostHog not to work out a location from it, and our PostHog project is set not to keep it.

When you buy credits, the extension opens Stripe's Checkout page (`checkout.stripe.com`) in a normal tab, as a link would. The extension sends nothing to Stripe itself: what you enter on that page goes to Stripe, and never to the extension or the worker. [Cofun's privacy page](../../PRIVACY.md#who-receives-your-data) says what Stripe and the worker exchange.

"Sign in" opens Cofun's sign-in page, `https://api.cofun.cc/login`, in a normal tab. What you enter there goes to Clerk and to the sign-in provider you choose, not to our worker; [Cofun's privacy page](../../PRIVACY.md#who-receives-your-data) says more. The side panel lets you sign out; it offers nothing to change the account.

## Usage data

Notico sends usage events to PostHog, a product analytics service, so that we can see how it is used: whether people who open the panel get as far as asking a question, which tools are used and how often they fail, and which of the panel's actions are used at all. **It is on until you turn it off**: open the settings from the account menu and switch off "Share usage data". While it is off, the analytics code is not started and nothing is sent to PostHog; switching it off takes effect at once, and an event that did not reach PostHog is never sent again later.

An event says that something happened, never what was written or read. Every value in it is a name from a fixed list, a number, or yes/no, and the code refuses to send an event that holds anything else. These are all the events:

| Event | What it holds |
|---|---|
| The panel was opened; the sign-in page was opened; you signed in | Nothing more |
| Your device's random id was joined to your account (`$identify`, sent by the analytics code when it first sees you signed in) | The random id and your Clerk user id |
| A question was asked | Which service answers (Cofun's hosted model, or OpenRouter), and whether the current tab was a Notion page |
| A question ended | The same service; how it ended (answered, stopped, out of steps, or failed, with our server's error code if it had one); how many times the model was called; how many tools ran |
| A tool ran | The tool's name (such as `read_page`), whether it worked and how it ended, and Notion's kind of error if it failed (such as "no access") |
| The model was switched; a key of your own was saved | Which service. Never the key, or the model's name |
| The settings were opened; the credits page was opened; the credit history was opened; a pack of credits was chosen to buy | For a pack, its size |
| A chat was started, opened, or deleted; a page was added to a question; a reply was stopped or copied | For an added page, whether with `@` or the "+" button. Never a chat's title, a page, or the reply |

Every event also holds the name of the extension (`notico`) and its version, the time, a random id of its own, the public key of our PostHog project, the name and version of the analytics code, the name and version of your browser and the name of your operating system, a random id for the stretch of use it belongs to, an instruction to PostHog not to work out a location, and who it is counted under, with whether that is an account: a random id made on your device, and once you are signed in your Clerk user id, to which that random id is joined, so that what happened before and after signing in is counted as one person. It holds no URL, no page title, not your name or email address, and not your time zone, language, or screen size.

Notico does not record clicks, keystrokes, or the screen, does not use PostHog's session replay, automatic event capture, or error reporting, and loads no code from PostHog: the analytics code is part of the extension's package. [PostHog's privacy policy](https://posthog.com/privacy) says how PostHog handles the data it is sent. When a payment for credits goes through, our worker also tells PostHog so; [Cofun's privacy page](../../PRIVACY.md#who-receives-your-data) says what that holds.

## What the worker does with it

The worker checks who you are, passes the conversation to the AI model and streams the reply back, keeps your credits, and keeps a usage record of each request: numbers, times, and ids, never what you asked or what the assistant answered. It does not store the conversation or the Notion content in it, and of a request that fails it logs at most a short, bounded part of the error. [Cofun's privacy page](../../PRIVACY.md#what-the-worker-keeps) has the full account: what is kept, for how long, and who can see it.

The side panel's account menu shows how many credits are left, and its Credits page shows them by where they came from, when they expire or renew, and how many you used on each of the last 30 days; older usage is not shown. From the Credits page you can open the history of your credits: every grant you were given, with what a purchase cost. The history is read from the worker only when you open it.

## What stays on your device

- **Your chats.** Chats are saved in the extension's IndexedDB storage in your Chrome profile, one database per signed-in account. They include tool results, such as the content of Notion pages the assistant read, the URL and title of a tab it looked up, the text of a page on another site it read, text you sent with "Ask Notico", the address and text of web pages it read for you, and the queries and results of its web searches; and, for each tab you added to a question, its title and URL then, Chrome's number for the tab, and a random name for that run of the browser, which the extension keeps in the browser's memory (`chrome.storage.session`) until the browser closes, so a tab added before a restart is not taken for another. When the assistant reads a long page from a tab or the web, the chat keeps its full text, up to 2,000,000 characters, also the parts the assistant never read, with the page's title and URL, so it can read further without fetching the page again; that text is deleted with the last chat that holds it, and text kept for a chat that was never saved is deleted a day later. Each chat also has a random id, and each question in it another: the extension sends both to our worker with the question's requests, so that what the worker recorded a question or a chat spent can be matched to it; they say nothing about the content. Chats are not sent to our servers, except that continuing a chat sends it to the worker again as described above. They are **not encrypted** and they **stay after you sign out**, so that you can pick up again: anyone with access to your Chrome profile can read them. Deleting a chat in the side panel removes it; removing the extension removes them all.
- **Your sign-in.** Clerk's code in the extension keeps its client token, which keeps you signed in, in the extension's local storage (`chrome.storage.local`). It is not encrypted either.
- **Your own model, if you set one up.** Your OpenRouter key, with whether it is switched on, the models you switched on there, and when it last turned a question away for the key, and the model chosen in the message box, are kept in the extension's local storage (`chrome.storage.local`), for the account that set them up: another account signed in on the same Chrome profile does not use them. They are **not encrypted** and they **stay after you sign out**; anyone with access to your Chrome profile can read the key. Switching OpenRouter off keeps the key; removing it in the settings page deletes it, and removing the extension deletes it too. A key is saved only after OpenRouter said it works.
- **Small settings.** The extension's local storage also holds, for each account, whether the assistant may change your pages (Edit or Read only); and for the device, whether usage data is shared, once you have changed it. Whether the right-click menu shows "Ask Notico", once you have changed it, is kept in Chrome's synced extension storage (`chrome.storage.sync`): if you use Chrome sync, Chrome takes that one on/off value, with the rest of what it syncs for your profile, to your other devices through Google's sync service.
- **The ids usage is counted under.** While usage data is on, the analytics code keeps, in the extension's own page storage (`localStorage`), the random id made for your device, the random id of the current stretch of use, and, once you are signed in, your Clerk user id. They are sent with usage events as described under [Usage data](#usage-data). It keeps its own bookkeeping there too, which is not sent: times, counts of the events sent, and the side panel page's own address inside the extension. After you turn usage data off they stay on the device and are sent nowhere. Removing the extension deletes them.

## What is never collected

- No conversation content in usage data: what Notico tells PostHog about how it is used never holds your messages, the assistant's replies, Notion content, a page's title or URL, or a key (see [Usage data](#usage-data)), and you can turn it off.
- No advertising, no tracking across sites or other extensions, no session recording, and no recording of clicks or keystrokes. The extension loads no remote code.
- The worker does not store Notion content, messages, or replies, and does not write them to its logs (of a failed model call or an unexpected failure it logs only a short, bounded part of the error, as described above).
- No Notion credentials or cookies are sent to the worker or to Clerk.
- No browsing history. Of the tabs that are not Notion, only the current tab, when the assistant looks it up to answer a question, and the tabs you add to a chat can leave the browser: their URLs and titles, and their pages' text only on a site you allowed. Text you select is read only when you send it with "Ask Notico", and leaves the browser only with your question.
- We do not sell your data, and we do not use it for anything other than providing the assistant.

## Changes and contact

This file is written by kind of data and by recipient. It changes in the same pull request as a change that adds a kind of data that leaves the browser, a host it goes to, or something kept on your device; its [history](https://github.com/cofun-labs/cofun/commits/main/extensions/notico/PRIVACY.md) shows every change. Questions and reports: [open an issue](https://github.com/cofun-labs/cofun/issues). Report a security vulnerability privately instead, as [SECURITY.md](../../SECURITY.md) says.
